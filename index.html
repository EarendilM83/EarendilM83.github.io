0.1 · The product in one paragraph

10x LMS is a vocational learning platform for schools and training centers. Students enrol in one of four programs — 💻 Programming (Python foundations), 👨‍🍳 Culinary (HACCP & kitchen operations), 🛎️ Hospitality (front-desk operations), 📊 Management (team leadership). Each program is a path of units; each unit is a tower of floors (short lessons of 4–8 questions, ~4 minutes) ending in a boss gate. On top of that path sits a Duolingo-grade gamified practice layer — streaks, dual currency, leagues, quests, chests, a shop, mascots — whose entire purpose is one thing: make the student come back tomorrow and make every minute measurably build skill. Web-first, mobile-first, institutional (no ads, no pay-to-win, ministry-auditable).

0.4 · Axis 2: four engagement archetypes — from real cohorts of 300–500/year

Calibrated from enrolment data: who actually disengages, when, and why. Shares are pilot-calibration estimates except the Driven Few, which is measured fact (20–25 of 300). Every archetype maps to named mechanics in 0.5 — none is left to "teacher heroics".

⚔️ The Confident Skipper · ~15–20% est.

Has partial background (incl. dropouts from other courses — whose confidence in foundations often exceeds their actual foundations). Skips basic lectures, disengages while waiting for "something special", and by the time content gets relevant he's not bonded to the community formed in month one — so he never comes back. Dropout signature: invisible disengagement weeks 2–6, then ghost. Core tension: we can't skip basics (others need them), and his confidence is partly illusion (Dunning–Kruger). Design response: N-02 prove-it floors + H-07 wager calibration + N-05 re-entry bridge + early cohort anchoring (N-03).

🌱 The Below-Zero Beginner · ~10–15% est.

Doesn't know Ctrl+C/Ctrl+V. Programming's absolute floor assumes computer literacy — he doesn't have it. Gives up at the first challenge, scared and demotivated, before any mechanic can help. Dropout signature: week-1 silent exit, often before day 3. Core tension: course basics start at zero; his zero is lower, and shame keeps him from asking. Design response: N-01 Floor 0 digital-literacy ramp (auto-placed, dignity-first framing) + guaranteed-win first floors + free hints in week 1.

⏰ The Parallel Worker · ~30–40% est.

Works alongside study; regularly misses lectures, mentoring sessions and community chat — so beyond the time scarcity (Part L) he suffers a belonging deficit: he never feels like a member of the space. Dropout signature: month-2 fade-out, "it wasn't really my world anyway". Core tension: community is built in synchronous moments he can't attend. Design response: Part L mechanics (whole time-fit layer) + N-03 async cohort anchor + Class Live recordings with async quest equivalents.

🎖️ The Driven Few · 20–25 of 300 (~7%, measured)

Understands the value of knowledge and pushes through every challenge. The school's hidden problem: even they need constant motivation, and today that motivation is teacher labour that doesn't scale. Dropout signature: they don't drop out — they burn out carrying others informally, unrewarded. Core tension: they're the community's backbone and nobody pays them for it. Design response: N-04 peer-mentor track — convert motivation cost into a rewarded role with status, coins and mentee wins.

0.5 · The first-month law — and the mechanics that enforce it

Institutional finding (measured): students who are not part of the community within the first month drift toward dropout — the dropout itself usually gets attributed to other reasons (work, money, "not for me"), but the root cause is the missing bond. Policy consequence: community attachment in the first 30 days is a product metric with a quality gate (6.2), not a nice-to-have — and every archetype above gets its anchor mechanic shipped in v1–v1.5, not "later".

J3 · The daily habit — day 2 to day 7, where products live or die

The first loop proves value; J3 turns it into a habit. The contract is simple and said to the student in plain words: one floor a day keeps the flame. Three surfaces carry J3 — the path (always the next action), the quests (steer the day), and the widget/notification (the outside-the-app reminder, governed by the notification law: max 2/day, behavior-triggered only, Part H).

1Opens from habit, widget, or nudge

Day 2 open lands on the path, streak counter incremented overnight, quests reset. No interstitials, no ads, no "rate us".

2Quests steer the session

S-13: three small goals. One always routes to a retention surface (clear a mistake, do a review) — by design, quests teach the whole loop.

3One floor, one payout

The J2 loop repeats with combo live from question one. Summary shows "streak N secured" — the daily contract fulfilled.

4Optional second floor

"One more?" is always available, never pushed. Daily XP cap (300⚡) silently prevents binge-grinding from wrecking the league economy.

523:40 · the save

If no floor today: one notification — "one floor saves 🔥N". Not two, not five. The law is the product's integrity.

6Day 7 · society knock

7-day streak → Streak Society Ember tier, flame skin unlock. The number becomes identity (Part H, H-09).

J4 · Mastery & review — the loop that makes it learning, not just habit

A streak without mastery is a vanity metric, and the ministry knows it. J4 is where 10x proves pedagogy: every wrong answer is captured (mistakes inbox), every skill fades on a schedule (decay radar), and review is scheduled against forgetting (SRS, Part H4.2). A partial write produces the worst bug class in this product — XP without progress, a streak without a lesson, a metric without an event.
